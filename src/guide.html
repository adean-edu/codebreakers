CODEBREAKERS · FOR TEACHERS

The Hidden War

A WWII intelligence game for high school history. Each student plays one of five intelligence roles across six rounds (1939–1944). In each round they crack a short cipher, make a decision, and see what really happened. Then they swap Signal IDs with classmates on Padlet to rebuild six secret messages, which they hand in to you privately.

About the name: version 1 was called "Codebreakers: The Silent Service". It is now "Codebreakers: The Hidden War", because "the Silent Service" is the well-known nickname of the submarine service, and the game is about the whole hidden war of codebreakers, couriers, double agents and liaison officers.

BEFORE CLASS

  1. Make your class Padlet in one click: open the ready-made Padlet while signed in to Padlet. It makes your own copy, with a column for each role, the pinned welcome post and an example card. None of another class’s posts come with it.
  2. Edit the welcome post’s last step to say how your students hand in their submission (it names the original teacher’s system).
  3. Check two settings in your copy: Attribution (author names) is on, and students must sign in with their school accounts. Then every card shows who posted it, even if a student types a codename. Comments (replies) should be on too.
  4. Prefer to build your own? Any Wall or Shelf Padlet works. A suggested welcome post is below.
  5. Decide how students hand in their submission: your learning management system, a form, or email. It must be private. Never on Padlet.

Suggested welcome post (copy and edit):

Welcome, codebreakers.
1. Play the game: choose any role and finish all six rounds.
2. Post ONLY your case card here: the card image plus the post text the game gives you.
3. Decode classmates from the OTHER four roles: type the Signal ID from their card into the game's Decoder board.
4. Reply to each card you decode with the reply the game writes for you. It starts with DECODED and their ID.
5. When all six messages are decoded, hand in your submission privately. Never post the messages here.

IN CLASS

  1. Students choose their own role and type a name or codename. Every role has a different piece of each message, so a mix of roles across the class is what makes the puzzle work. If one role is missing, students cannot finish; nudge a few students toward it.
  2. They play six rounds. Each round has a briefing, a cipher to crack with the dial, a decision, and a debrief comparing their choice with what really happened. Plan for at least one class period.
  3. They post their card on Padlet: a card image (download or copy) plus the post text, which includes their Signal ID and a question for classmates.
  4. They decode classmates. On the Decoder board they type Signal IDs from cards of the four other roles. Each one shows that student's choices and unlocks their pieces of the messages. They reply to each card with the reply text the game writes.
  5. They hand in the six messages. When all six are complete, the game makes a submission with their name, their own Signal ID, the Signal IDs they decoded, their verdicts, and a short reflection. They copy it and hand it in privately.

TRUST OR TRAITOR

This is the assessment part of the game. Students check each other's history facts, all with choices only and no typing.

  1. A secret choice. After round 6, each student chooses in secret to be a loyal agent or a double agent. A double agent picks one of three intel reports to falsify, and the game writes the lie. The choice is hidden inside their Signal ID. Nobody else can see it.
  2. Intel reports on Padlet. The post text carries three short intel reports from the student's front. If the student is a double agent, one report contains the lie.
  3. The interrogation. When a student decodes a classmate, the game asks three questions: approve or deny the reports; if deny, which report is false; then "prove it" by picking the truth from three choices. A verdict can't be changed once it is filed. Verdicts never appear on Padlet, so nobody can see who has already been caught.
  4. The Archive. The Archive button opens declassified files, one per fact, filed in drawers by role. Key details are blacked out. Students hover over a black bar, or tap it, to see what is underneath. Reading is free, but revealing a blacked-out line while an interrogation is open marks that verdict "with research".
  5. Sealed results. Students see whether they were right only after they have decoded all six messages. The results then appear above their submission.

Scoring. A verdict earns 1 point if it is right. On a double agent, it earns 1 more point for naming the false report, and 1 more for picking the truth. A perfect verdict made "with research" scores 2 instead of 3. As agents, loyal students score 1 point for each classmate who approved them, and double agents score 1 point for each classmate they fooled. Teacher Check works out every score again from the Signal IDs, so students can't change their own scores.

The Unmasking. After grading, press Show the Unmasking in Teacher Check. It shows the class who was a double agent, the best deceiver, the best spy-catcher, and every planted lie with the truth beside it. Keep it hidden until everyone has finished.

GRADING

  1. Collect the submissions and paste them, all at once, into Teacher Check.
  2. Each student gets a row: whether their own Signal ID is real, which of the five roles they covered, whether the six messages are right, their Trust or Traitor verdict points and research count, their points as an agent, and anything to look at (made-up Signal IDs, two students with the same Signal ID, reflections copied word for word).
  3. Use Copy results for a spreadsheet to paste the table into your gradebook. Look up one Signal ID shows the choices behind any card on the Padlet.

Known limits: Signal IDs are checked, but they are not tamper-proof, and a Signal ID from another class works in any class. Teacher Check flags the most common shortcuts; it does not replace your judgment.

PRIVACY

  • No accounts and no data collection. The game is a single web page. It sends nothing to any server.
  • Student names are never saved. A student's name is kept only while the page is open, for their card and their submission. The game remembers progress and decoded Signal IDs in the student's own browser, without the name.
  • No outside connections. Fonts and pictures are built into the page. It is hosted on GitHub Pages.
  • Padlet is your own tool. Its privacy settings and your school's rules apply there.

HISTORICAL ACCURACY

Every Real History box, briefing and map was fact-checked in November 2026 against institutional sources (the NSA's Center for Cryptologic History, the UK National Archives, the US Naval History and Heritage Command, the Imperial War Museums, Bletchley Park, and standard histories). The players' choices are what-if decisions, not claims about what happened; each round's "real call" says what really happened. The scenes are symbolic pixel art.
