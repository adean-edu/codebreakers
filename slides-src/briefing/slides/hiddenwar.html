The concepts

The hidden war

Behind the battles of 1939 to 1945, a second war was fought with radio messages, ciphers and lies.

1

Break the codes

Germany and Japan sent their orders by radio, in code. Allied codebreakers learned to read many of them.

In history: Polish mathematicians broke Enigma in 1932 and shared their methods with Britain and France in July 1939.

2

Hide the source

If the enemy suspected, it would change its codes. So every secret needed a believable cover story.

In history: Britain called this intelligence Ultra. The secret was kept until 1974, almost 30 years after the war.

3

Fool the enemy

Double agents and fake plans fed the enemy lies, so it prepared for attacks in the wrong places.

In history: In 1943, a dead body carrying fake plans helped convince Germany the Allies would land in Greece and Sardinia, not Sicily.

Most people think of World War II as tanks, planes and soldiers. But there was also a hidden war of information. The Allies broke Germany's Enigma cipher and Japan's naval codes. The hard part was using what they learned without giving the secret away, and fooling the enemy with false information. You will see all three of these ideas in the game. Each card has a real example: the Polish codebreakers who first broke Enigma, the Ultra secret that Britain kept until 1974, and Operation Mincemeat, when a dead body carrying fake invasion plans fooled Germany in 1943.
