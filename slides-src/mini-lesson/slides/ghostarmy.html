The Ghost Army

A US Army unit of about 1,100 soldiers whose job was to look like a much bigger army.

  • Fake tanks: inflatable rubber tanks and trucks that looked real from the air.
  • Fake sounds: huge speakers played recordings of tanks and troops at night.
  • Real artists: including future fashion designer Bill Blass and painter Ellsworth Kelly.

From June 1944 to March 1945 they staged more than 20 deceptions across Europe. In 2024 the unit received the Congressional Gold Medal.

An inflatable dummy Sherman tank, the kind of decoy the Ghost Army used. Photo: Imperial War Museums, public domain.

The Ghost Army was a real US Army unit of about 1,100 soldiers, and its job was to fool the Germans into seeing armies that weren't there. The photo shows an inflatable dummy Sherman tank, the kind of decoy they used: from the air it looked real. They also played recordings of tanks and soldiers through huge speakers at night, and sent fake radio messages. Many of them were artists and designers, including Bill Blass, who later became a famous fashion designer, and the painter Ellsworth Kelly. Between June 1944 and March 1945 they carried out more than 20 deception missions across France, Belgium, Luxembourg and Germany. Their work stayed secret for decades, and in 2024 the unit was awarded the Congressional Gold Medal. In the game, the fake army before D-Day uses the same kind of trick.
