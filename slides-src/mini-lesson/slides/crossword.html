Here's a true story. In January 1942, readers wrote to the Daily Telegraph newspaper to complain that its crossword was too easy. So a challenge was set up: solve a crossword under timed conditions in 12 minutes. Some of the people who finished in time were later quietly contacted and invited to more tests, and some went on to do secret codebreaking work. Crosswords use the same skills as codebreaking: spotting patterns, guessing a word from a few letters, and staying calm under pressure. Ask: would you have made it?
