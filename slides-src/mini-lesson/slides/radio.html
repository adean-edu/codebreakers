The Resistance Courier role in the game faces the same danger: every message is a risk.

Noor Inayat Khan, 1943. Photo: Imperial War Museums, public domain.

Agents in occupied Europe needed a way to talk to London, and that job belonged to secret radio operators. They tapped out Morse code on radio sets hidden in suitcases, while German detector vans drove the streets trying to find their signals, so they had to keep moving and stay on the air as briefly as possible. Noor Inayat Khan, shown here in 1943, code name Madeleine, became the first woman radio operator sent into occupied France by Britain's Special Operations Executive, in June 1943. When her network was broken up, she refused to leave, and for a time she was one of the last links between Paris and London. She was betrayed and arrested in October 1943, and killed at Dachau concentration camp in 1944. She never revealed her secrets. She was awarded the George Cross, Britain's highest civilian award for bravery, after the war. This is a serious story; it shows how dangerous this work was.
