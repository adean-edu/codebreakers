Here is a real codebreaking mystery that is still unsolved. In 2012, a man cleaning a chimney in a house in Bletchingley, England, found the skeleton of a pigeon with a small red canister on its leg. Inside was a coded message from World War II: 27 groups of five letters. Britain's codebreakers at GCHQ, the modern descendant of Bletchley Park, tried and said they could not read it without the original codebook. Pigeons carried many messages in the war, and 32 were awarded the Dickin Medal, a medal for animal bravery. Discussion question: why might a code that was only used once be almost impossible to break? It connects to the game: a cipher is only as safe as its key.
