Virginia Hall: the limping lady

  • An American from Baltimore, she lost part of her left leg in a 1933 hunting accident and walked on an artificial leg she nicknamed “Cuthbert.”
  • From 1941, working for Britain’s SOE, she organized resistance networks in occupied France, finding safe houses and helping agents escape.
  • The Gestapo hunted her. In late 1942 she escaped by crossing the Pyrenees mountains on foot in winter.
  • She came back in 1944 for the American OSS to train resistance fighters around the time of D-Day.
  • She was the only civilian woman awarded the US Distinguished Service Cross in World War II.

Virginia Hall receiving the Distinguished Service Cross, September 1945. Photo: CIA, public domain.

Virginia Hall was an American from Baltimore. After losing part of her left leg in a hunting accident, she walked on an artificial leg she called Cuthbert. Even so, she became one of the most important Allied agents in occupied France. Working first for Britain's Special Operations Executive, she built resistance networks, found safe houses and helped agents escape. The Gestapo hunted her as "the limping lady." In late 1942 she escaped over the Pyrenees mountains to Spain on foot, in winter. She went back to France in 1944 for the American OSS to train resistance fighters around D-Day. This photo shows her receiving the Distinguished Service Cross in 1945: she was the only civilian woman to receive it in World War II. In the game, she is one of the real people behind the Resistance Courier role.
