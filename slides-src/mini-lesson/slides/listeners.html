More roles in the secret war

The listeners

  • Before anyone could break a code, someone had to catch the message. Britain’s “Y Service” listeners tuned in to enemy radio day and night.
  • They wrote down every Morse code letter by hand, even though the messages looked like nonsense to them.
  • Many were young women in the women’s services of the navy, army and air force.
  • The messages went to Bletchley Park by motorcycle courier and teleprinter.
  • One missed letter could make a message impossible to break, so accuracy mattered more than speed.

In the game, every intercept you decrypt was first caught by someone like this.

Codebreakers could only break what someone had first heard. The Y Service was Britain's network of radio listening stations. Operators, many of them young women in the women's branches of the navy, army and air force, sat with headphones for hours, writing down enemy Morse code letter by letter. The photo shows a WAAF wireless operator doing exactly that kind of work. The messages meant nothing to them, because they were in code, but one missed letter could make a message much harder to break. The intercepts were rushed to Bletchley Park by motorcycle dispatch riders and later by teleprinter. Most of these listeners never knew what happened to the messages they took down.
