The man who never was

  • To hide the real target, Sicily, British officers Ewen Montagu and Charles Cholmondeley planned to fool Germany with a dead body carrying fake invasion plans.
  • The body was a homeless Welshman, Glyndwr Michael, given a whole fake life.
  • His pockets held theater ticket stubs, love letters from “Pam” with her photo (really an MI5 clerk), and a bank letter about his overdraft.
  • On 30 April 1943 a British submarine released him off Spain, and a Spanish fisherman found him.
  • When Germany fell for it, Churchill was told: “Mincemeat swallowed rod, line and sinker.”

FORGED

The fake ID card of “Major William Martin.” The photo isn’t the dead man: it’s an MI5 officer, Ronnie Reed, who looked like him. Source: Ewen Montagu, 1953, public domain.

This is one of the most famous tricks of the war, and it's in the game. In 1943 the Allies were about to invade Sicily, and Germany knew Sicily was the obvious target. British intelligence officers Ewen Montagu and Charles Cholmondeley came up with a plan: a dead body dressed as a British officer, carrying fake letters about invading Greece and Sardinia instead. The body was a homeless Welshman named Glyndwr Michael. They gave "Major William Martin" a whole life: theater ticket stubs, love letters from a fiancée called Pam, with a photo that was really of an MI5 clerk, even a letter from his bank about money he owed. Look at the ID card: the photo on it isn't the dead man at all, but an MI5 officer, Ronnie Reed, who looked a bit like him. A British submarine released the body off the coast of Spain on 30 April 1943, and a Spanish fisherman found it. German intelligence believed the papers and sent reinforcements to Greece and Sardinia. Churchill was told: "Mincemeat swallowed rod, line and sinker."
