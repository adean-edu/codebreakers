Most people picture codebreakers as men in suits, but most of the codebreaking workforce was women. By 1945 nearly 10,000 people worked at Bletchley Park and its outstations, and about three-quarters of them were women. They ran the bombe machines, sorted and translated messages, and broke codes themselves. In the United States, more than 10,000 women worked as Army and Navy codebreakers. They signed oaths of secrecy, and many kept the secret for decades, even from their own families.
