Hiding a secret in plain sight: the same idea behind every cover story in the game.

Background: a WWII silk escape map. MI9 made maps like this; this one is American. Photo: CIA Museum, public domain.

MI9 was the British department that helped prisoners of war escape and helped airmen shot down over Europe get home. Its gadget makers, led by the inventive Christopher Clayton Hutton, came up with all sorts of tricks. Escape maps were printed on silk, like the one in the photo, which doesn't rustle when searched, survives water, and folds small enough to hide in a boot heel. This particular map was made by the American OSS, which copied the idea. Tiny compasses were hidden in buttons, pens and razors. Maps, compasses and real money were even smuggled into prison camps hidden inside Monopoly game boards, sent in parcels from made-up charities so the Germans wouldn't suspect them. Some prisoners used secret codes in their letters home to send information to Britain. It was all about hiding a secret in plain sight.
