More roles in the secret war

The coastwatchers

Coastwatchers outside their radio hut on Guadalcanal. Photo: Australian War Memorial, public domain.

  • Australian and British officers, helped by local islanders, hid in the hills and jungles of islands like the Solomons, behind enemy lines.
  • When Japanese planes or ships passed, they radioed a warning, giving Allied fighters time to get ready.
  • Their warnings helped the Allies hold on at Guadalcanal in 1942.
  • In 1943, islanders Biuku Gasa and Eroni Kumana found the stranded crew of PT-109, including future president John F. Kennedy, and coastwatchers arranged their rescue.

In the game, the cover story after Yamamoto’s plane is shot down points to coastwatchers.

The coastwatchers were Allied lookouts hidden on Pacific islands that Japan had captured. The photo shows a group of them outside their radio hut on Guadalcanal. Most were Australian or British officers who knew the islands well, and they depended completely on local islanders for food, guides and protection. From hiding places in the hills, they radioed warnings whenever Japanese planes or ships went past, which gave Allied pilots time to get into the air. Their warnings were a big help during the long battle for Guadalcanal in 1942. In 1943, two islanders, Biuku Gasa and Eroni Kumana, found the stranded crew of the torpedo boat PT-109, including a young officer named John F. Kennedy, and coastwatchers arranged the rescue. In the game, the Americans let people believe coastwatchers had spotted Yamamoto, to hide that the code had been broken.
